# Principal Product Designer

## About BlackHyve

We're building the system contractors use to run a profitable business. Construction is a $2 trillion industry, and the best contractors aren't 10% more profitable than average. They're 3 to 11x more profitable. The difference comes down to knowing what's actually happening in the field, comparing it to the plan, and acting before margin quietly disappears.

BlackHyve closes that gap. We connect planning, production, scheduling, field reporting, and financial visibility in one product so contractors can understand what's happening while there's still time to do something about it.

That means BlackHyve is a broad product. A foreman might spend most of the day in our mobile app. A superintendent moves between mobile and web. A project manager may live in schedules, budgets, production data, and reporting. An executive needs to understand the same underlying information from a completely different altitude.

We think that breadth should be a strength. Learn an interaction in one part of BlackHyve and it should feel familiar everywhere else. Complex information should feel understandable. The product should feel coherent even when the jobs people are doing are very different.

Today, we're not nearly good enough at that.

We're looking for a Principal Product Designer to help change it.

## The role

You'll define the design language of BlackHyve.

This isn't primarily a role for taking requirements and turning them into polished screens. We need someone who can look across the entire product, find the patterns hiding inside it, and establish the systems that make BlackHyve feel like one product instead of a collection of features.

A major part of the job will be building and evolving our design system: typography, hierarchy, spacing, color, components, interaction patterns, states, navigation, responsive behavior, and the principles underneath them. More importantly, you'll work directly with our engineers to make those patterns real in the product and reusable by the people building it.

You'll also work on product problems directly. BlackHyve contains dense, information-heavy interfaces: schedules, budgets, production data, quantities, crews, activities, reports, and more. Often the challenge isn't figuring out how to display something. It's figuring out what matters, what doesn't, and how to make a lot of information understandable without making the product feel sterile or overwhelming.

You'll be a sounding board for new product ideas and new interaction patterns. Sometimes you'll design the solution yourself. Sometimes you'll establish the pattern and help an engineer apply it. Sometimes you'll tell us we're solving the wrong problem.

And you'll help us develop a recognizable point of view. Construction software doesn't have to be gray, joyless enterprise software. Our customers are serious people doing serious work, but that doesn't mean the tools they use can't have personality, clarity, and moments of delight.

## What you'd work on in your first 3–6 months

We can't predict the exact product priorities six months from now, but the initial mandate is unusually clear:

- **Establish the foundations of a BlackHyve design system.** Audit the existing product, identify the primitives and recurring patterns we already have, decide what should survive, and create the system we're going to build on.
- **Create consistency across a wide product.** Establish common patterns for navigation, selection, filtering, editing, tables, forms, status, hierarchy, actions, and other interactions so users don't have to relearn BlackHyve every time they enter a different part of the product.
- **Take on one of our hardest information-design problems.** Work through a dense, data-heavy surface and demonstrate what it looks like when information hierarchy, interaction design, and visual design all work together.
- **Work with engineers to get the system into production.** We don't want a beautiful Figma library that slowly diverges from the application. You'll collaborate directly with the people building BlackHyve so the design system becomes part of how we ship software.
- **Set the visual direction for the product.** Help us establish the typography, color, density, motion, personality, and small details that make BlackHyve recognizably BlackHyve.
- **Raise our design judgment as a team.** Give engineers and product people principles they can use when you're not in the room, and become the person we pull into a problem when a new interaction doesn't have an obvious answer.

## What we're looking for

- **You've designed complex software products.** You understand applications where users do real work all day, not just marketing sites or simple consumer flows.
- **You think in systems.** You've built or substantially shaped a design system before, or you've worked deeply enough within one that you're ready to own the whole thing. You understand that a design system isn't a component library; it's a shared language for building a product.
- **You're exceptional at information hierarchy.** You can take a screen containing an uncomfortable amount of information and make the important things obvious without simply hiding everything else.
- **You care about interaction design as much as visual design.** How something behaves, what users learn from it, and whether that knowledge transfers elsewhere in the product matters as much as how the final screen looks.
- **You have strong product judgment.** You can work from an incomplete problem, ask the right questions, explore possibilities, cut scope, and arrive at something that should actually be built.
- **You work well with engineers.** You understand enough about how software gets built to design things that can become durable product patterns. You want a tight feedback loop with engineering rather than a design handoff.
- **You have taste and a point of view.** You're willing to push beyond the default SaaS answer when there's a better one, while knowing when novelty would just get in the user's way.
- **You can operate independently.** We're a small team. We need someone who can identify design problems and move them forward without waiting for a perfectly formed brief.
- **You care about the details.** Copy, empty states, loading states, keyboard behavior, responsiveness, accessibility, animation, spacing — the little things accumulate into how a product feels.
- **You're curious about using AI in your work.** We use AI to explore alternatives, prototype interactions, and move through iteration faster, while keeping design judgment in the hands of the person doing the work. Prior AI experience isn't a hiring requirement. We can teach the tools and workflows to someone with exceptional design taste and product judgment; we'd like you to be willing to learn and help us discover where they actually improve the product.

You don't need to check every box because you've already done this exact job before. We would also be excited about someone who has spent years doing excellent product design inside a strong system and is ready for the next step: owning the system and design direction themselves.

What matters is being able to show us the judgment, craft, and systems thinking to do it.

## Product, not just pixels

We're especially interested in designers who blur some traditional boundaries.

Maybe you can prototype interactions in code. Maybe you're comfortable working directly with frontend engineers on implementation. Maybe you're a great product writer. Maybe you've done brand work and can help connect the personality of BlackHyve the company with BlackHyve the product.

You don't need to be all of those things.

But we want a designer who sees the finished product as the work — not the Figma file.

There's also an opportunity to influence BlackHyve's broader brand. Product design is the primary job, but we'd love someone who can help us develop a visual language that carries naturally between the application, our website, product launches, and the way we present ourselves to the world.

## What makes this different

- **You get to define the system, not inherit it.** There are pieces worth keeping, but we don't have a mature design system that you're being hired to maintain. You'll have enormous influence over what BlackHyve looks and feels like for years.
- **The design problem is genuinely difficult.** Different people, devices, environments, levels of technical comfort, and enormous amounts of interconnected information all live inside one product. Making that feel simple without making it simplistic is the job.
- **There's room for personality.** Construction software has traditionally set an extraordinarily low bar for design. We don't think our customers need another generic enterprise application.
- **You'll work directly with the people building the product.** We're a small team. There isn't a chain of product managers, design managers, and engineering managers between an idea and the person making it real.
- **The product is still malleable.** We have paying customers and a broad product, but we're early enough that fundamental patterns aren't calcified yet. You can change them.
- **Real industry, real problems.** Our users build things in the physical world. The information BlackHyve presents can change what happens on a jobsite tomorrow, not just whether someone clicks a button.

## How this role starts

Finding the right person matters more to us than forcing the role into a particular employment arrangement.

Our preference may be to start working together on a fractional or part-time basis. That gives both sides a chance to work on real BlackHyve problems together and see whether the fit is as good in practice as it looks on paper.

For the right person, we'd be excited for that relationship to grow into a full-time role.

If you're already looking for full-time work and this sounds exactly like the problem you want to solve, we'd still like to hear from you. We're more interested in finding the right designer than prescribing the arrangement in advance.

## Logistics

- **Work model:** Fully remote. We don't have an office. We get together in person at least once a year for an on-site.
- **Engagement:** We're open to fractional, part-time, or full-time depending on the person.
- **Benefits:** For full-time employees: health insurance, paid time off, and an equipment stipend.
- **Compensation:** For full-time employment, $145,000–$225,000 base salary in the US, adjusted by location using our [compensation policy](compensation.md). Part-time salary is prorated to the agreed schedule; fractional consulting fees are agreed separately. We'll discuss this early and structure it appropriately for the engagement.
- **Equity:** For a full-time role, there's an equity component at this stage of the company. Specifics are part of the offer conversation.

## How to apply

Send your application to **apply@blackhyve.com** with:

- A short note telling us why this problem interests you.
- Links to two or three pieces of product work you're proud of.
- For at least one of them, show us more than the final screens. We'd like to understand the problem, the constraints, the decisions you made, what shipped, and what you'd change now.
- If you've built or significantly contributed to a design system, show us that too.
- Two or three references we can talk to.

We're much more interested in seeing how you think through real product problems than in a perfectly polished portfolio.

## How we hire

We respect senior candidates' time.

1. **Application review.** We look closely at the work you send and the thinking behind it.
2. **Conversation and past-work deep dive.** About an hour. We'll talk about your work, BlackHyve, and the design problems we're trying to solve. You'll walk us through one or two substantial projects, including the messy parts: constraints, alternatives, disagreements, tradeoffs, and what actually shipped.
3. **Work through a real BlackHyve problem together.** Rather than give you a speculative design exercise, we'll open up an actual part of the product and talk through it together. We're interested in how you see the problem, what you notice, and how you collaborate.
4. **Meet more of the team.** You'll talk with the engineers and people you'd work closely with.
5. **References and next steps.** We talk to the references you sent us and, if there's a fit, figure out the right way to start working together.

No design homework. No unpaid take-home project. We'd rather spend time looking at work you've actually done and solve a real problem together.
