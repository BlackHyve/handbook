# Lead Product Engineer

## About BlackHyve

We're building the operating system for construction production. The industry is $2 trillion and still runs on spreadsheets, PDFs, and software designed in the 1990s. The result: subcontractors, general contractors, and fabricators lose margin they shouldn't, and nobody can see it happening until the job is already underwater.

BlackHyve gives construction teams real-time production visibility: what was estimated, what's actually happening on site, where drift is hiding, and how to recover before margin disappears.

We're a small team of engineers, sales, and forward-deployed folks who all work close to the product and the customer. Our CTO is deep in product and AI; our CEO spent two decades building construction companies. Our pipeline is bigger than we can serve today and we're hiring a Lead Product Engineer to help close that gap.

## The role

You'll own a substantial part of the product end-to-end — a subsystem, not a feature. One quarter that might be the mobile capture experience, the next it might be the executive briefing layer, the next it might be the scheduling engine. The scope shifts with what's driving revenue.

Inside your area, you set the bar. You decide the architecture, you absorb the rough edges, you talk to the customers using it, and you set the engineering standards your peers operate by when they touch your code.

You'll ship features end-to-end, from "this is what the customer needs" to "this is in production and people use it every day."

You'll work cross-functionally by default. Talking to customers is part of the job. Sitting in on a sales call is part of the job. Shaping the problem before you write a line of code is part of the job.

You'll lead the AI-assisted work happening on your subsystem. We're already an AI-pilled org and moving toward putting more compute off the developer's machine, not just on it. We want a Lead who has opinions about how AI changes the way their area gets built and runs.

## What you'd ship in your first 3–6 months

We can't predict exactly what will be hot in three months, but here's a representative sample of the kinds of projects a Lead here owns end-to-end:

- **Take ownership of a substantial subsystem.** Pick up an area like the mobile capture experience, the scheduling engine, or the executive briefing layer, and operate it — architecture, roadmap, the rough edges, the customer feedback loop.
- **Ship a cross-stack customer-blocking feature.** Something that touches mobile, API, and backend together. You write the code, you talk to the customers, you operate it in production.
- **Build an AI-agent-assisted feature on your surface.** Schedule extraction, drift classification, executive briefings — pick one, ship it agent-first, and bring your peers along on what worked.
- **Lead a substantial redesign of a customer-facing surface.** Sit with two or three customers, identify the highest-leverage UX gap, and ship the redesign from research through release.

## What we're looking for

- **Deep expertise in at least one of:** frontend (React), backend (Laravel/PHP, TypeScript), or mobile (React Native). We expect you to be fluent in one of these and able to ramp up quickly in the others.
- **A track record of shipping substantial product end-to-end.** Not "I led a team that shipped." You wrote the code, you talked to the customers, you operated it in production.
- **Comfort in a small team.** We hire at this level for people who manage themselves and work shoulder-to-shoulder with other senior people. You figure out what needs doing without being asked, and you ship well inside a small group where collaboration is constant.
- **AI-native, or wanting to become AI-native fast.** You don't need to be a power user on day one, but you need to want to operate this way and have opinions about how AI changes how product engineers work.
- **Cross-functional instinct.** Comfortable in a code review, a customer call, a design review, and a sales call.
- **Taste.** You can tell the difference between "ships fast" and "ships sloppy."

## What makes this different

- **Real industry, real problems.** Construction is unsexy, which is why nobody's built this yet. The companies we sell to lose hundreds of thousands of dollars per project to problems we can actually solve. The work matters.
- **Small team, high autonomy, low theater.** No process layer cake, no two-week sprint ceremonies, no waiting on three approvals to ship. You'll work close to the CTO on technical direction and close to the CEO on customer interactions. Decisions get made by the people doing the work.
- **AI-pilled.** We treat AI as an amplifier, not a checkbox. We're rebuilding workflows around what's possible when an engineer + AI agents can ship what used to take a team of five.
- **Stage.** Past zero, with paying customers, real revenue, and growing pipeline, but still early enough that you'll have a defining hand in what BlackHyve becomes.
- **Ship daily.** We move fast and ship quality code to production every day.

## Career path

This role is the rung below Principal Product Engineer. On a team this size, you'll stretch into Principal-level work regularly — cross-product scope, setting patterns others follow, occasionally directing how AI gets used across engineering. Our Principal tier is open above this role. Promotion happens when you consistently operate at that scope, not after a calendar fires.

## Logistics

- **Work model:** Fully remote. We don't have an office. We get together in person at least once a year for an on-site.
- **Benefits:** Health insurance, paid time off, and an equipment stipend.
- **Compensation:** $135,000–$185,000 base salary in the US. Adjusted by location: US and major Canadian metros (Toronto, Vancouver, Montreal) at 100%; other Canada, Western Europe, Australia, New Zealand at 85%; rest of world at 60%. Equity grant scaled to stage and level. Our cash bands sit ~25% below comparable AI-pilled SaaS shops — we're transparent about it, and we close the gap as we grow.

## How we hire

We respect senior candidates' time.

1. **Application review.** We read what you sent: the cover letter, the ship links, the references. If there's a fit, we'll reach out within a week.
2. **Conversation with the CTO.** ~45 minutes. We dig into your work, your motivations, and your read on the problem space.
3. **Deep-dive on past work.** You walk us through one or two things you've shipped: the decisions, the tradeoffs, what you'd do differently.
4. **Join a working session.** Sit in on one of our team huddles and/or a shaping session and work through a real problem with us. We're not testing your code; we're seeing how you think about a problem.
5. **Conversation with the CEO.** ~45 minutes. Customer empathy, business fit, mutual gut-check.
6. **References and offer.** We talk to two or three of the people you sent us. If we're a fit, we make an offer.

Usually 2–3 weeks end-to-end.
